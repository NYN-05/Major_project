_**Abstract**_ **— The recent advent of extremely realistic**
**deepfakes generated by GANs and diffusion models has**
**presented an unsparing challenge to face-based authentication**
**and remote identity verification. Tradition Single-mode**
**detectors are lost during compression, after-processing, and to**
**unseen generator families and thus hurt inauthentic that**
**systems via imitation. In this paper, we propose Multimodal**
**Vision-Transformer** **Forensics** **(MViT-Forensics),** **a**
**transformer-based forensic framework luxurious for secure**
**authentication applied in deepfake Scenario. MViT-Forensics**
**processes spatial RGB embeddings (such as Swin/ViT**
**backbone), frequency domain residuals (DCT/FFT process step)**
**and physiological rPPG waveforms (attention-based temporal**
**encoder) together, and combines them using a cross-attention**
**multimodal transformer. The above anomaly-aware intrusion**
**scoring head will generate a confidence score in the form of**
**authenticity level for an authentication policy. We analyze the**
**approach on four public benchmarks—FaceForensics++,**
**Celeb-DF v2, DFDC Preview and the Multimodal DeepFake**
**Dataset (MMDF)— in standard and compressed conditions.**
**Our model reaches average accuracies of 98.1% (FF++), 96.4%**
**(Celeb-DF), 94.7% (DFDC) and 97.2% (MMDF) with mean**
**AUC ≈ 0:99, and maintains >90 percent accuracy at high levels**
**of compression while having better performance than strong**
**CNN** **or** **transformer** **baselines.** **Ablations** **verify** **the**
**complementary contributions in frequency and physiology**
**branches and the superiority of cross-attention fusion. Our**
**findings suggest that multimodal attention-based forensics with**
**intrusion scoring could close the gap between academic**
**detectors and operational authentication needs.**

I. INTRODUCTION

The emergence of generative adversarial networks
(GANs), diffusion models, and vision–language
transformers has rapidly advanced deepfake techniques,
greatly increasing the fidelity of manipulated facial content.
These synthetic videos and images can go on to reproduce
micro-expressions, merge lighting environments, maquerade
identity-conditional patterns more closely than have been
possible till now so that the fake is not just enough for its
intended purposes of identity spoofing and social engineering
but also extended fraud before high-risk authentication



systems. Traditional face-based authentication systems
(static-based facial recognition and variety levels of liveness
detection) find it difficult to distinguish between the real
input submitted by genuine users and spoofed synthetic
streams produced by state-of-the-art pipeline generating
deep fakes. Additionally, current forensic detectors often rely
on single-modality cues (i.e. pixel-level texture information,
motion nonuniformities or simple frequency artifacts)
becoming even more fragile when deepfake generation
models become proficient in hiding or suppressing the
evidence that may be found within visual indicators.


Another important limitation is the generalization gap
introduced by such detectors and several detectors fail when
testing on different generative models, compression artifacts
or post-processing pipelines not seen during training but
found in real-world authentication setups. This
inconsistency between the laboratory setting and real-world
deployment makes such systems vulnerable, since
adversaries can apply new generation methods or re-encode
videos to elude detectors. Furthermore, the physiological
cues, such as rPPG (natural color changes caused by
heartbeats) are missing, distorted or impossibly modeled in
deepfakes. However, conventional CNN-based forensic
methods are incapable of well processing these various
multimodal inconsistencies jointly.


To solve these problems, in this work we propose
Multimodal Vision Transformer Forensics for DeepfakeResistant Secure Authentication, a unified forensics
framework that unifies the representational power of vision
transformers (ViTs) to combine heterogeneous cues to
achieve strong security against attacks in authentication.
Unlike single-modality pipelines, the proposed model
consolidates three synergistic forensic domains: (1) spatialdomain features that capture fine-grained visual artifacts and
identity-specic textures; (2) frequency-domain cues that
unveil hidden spectral inconsistencies injected during
synthesis; and (3) physiological rPPG signals that expose
subtle heartbeat-related variations beyond what can be
realistically generated by deepfake generators. Upon these

II. LITERATURE SURVEY

The transformer-based model has turned out to be more
and more popular for recent deepfake detection with the
ability to capture global spatial dependencies as well as subtle
faked influences. It was shown early on that vision
transformers could beat CNN baselines in terms of
classification performance across benchmark datasets by
learning richer patch-level inconsistencies, demonstrated
through transformer-centric experiments like the DFDT
model [1]. Based on that, several hybrid CNN-Transformer
models have been introduced to fuse local texture extraction
with global attention features, thereby improving the
detection performance against image and video deepfakes in
identity verification usage [2]. In addition to spatial cues, the
researchers noted that the artifacts in deepfake can occur in
frequency domain which led to frequency-filtered residual
networks who are able to maintain discriminative spectral
patterns even after undergoing compression or downsampling [3]. To enhance generalization, frequency-domain
masking and spatial-interaction units were proposed to
regularise detectors learning intrinsic forgery patterns that are
non the less agnostic of emperical generative models [4].
More recent works, including FreqBlender took this concept
a step further by injecting spectral blend knowledge between
true and synthetic face data so that the detectors could be
trained to learn universal frequency type fingerprints
irrespective of generator drift [5].


Alongside spatial and frequency studies, physiologicalsignal-based deepfake detection has been emerging following
the synthetic challenge of biometric indicators such as heartrate-induced color changes (rPPG). Fourier-based modeling
of biological signaling were used to show that “oscillation
frequencies” related to skin tone could be used to distinguish
between real and synthetic faces within video [6]. Net survey
was followed by the comparison established that singlemodality methods suffer in practical scenarios such as
compression, domain translation and adversarial tampering
suggesting the need for multimodal fusion to achieve robust
forensics [7].


For the efficiency-sensitive scenario, lightweight
transformer-based architectures are also proposed to validate
the potential possibility to deploy well-designed attention
mechanisms for high-accuracy deepfake detection in real
time [8]. In addition to these methods, two-stream feature



domain fusion approaches that fuse the frequency and spatial
streams via cross-attention exhibited great robustness on lowquality and novel deepfakes [9]. It was observed that
universal artifact learning is further tightened by frequency
masking policies, which teach models to identify inherent
generative fallacies irrespective of the synthesis method, and
increases resistance against diffusion based deepfakes [10].


Meanwhile, multimodal transformer fusion in particular
audio-visual cross_attention – has proved that dynamic
weighting methods can enhance detection when features from
one modality are unreliable in unconstrained scenarios [11].
Finally, cross-architecture experiments across CNN, ViT,
Swin and CaiT further demonstrated that transformer-based
models generally achieve better generalization performance
for cross-dataset adaptation but still exhibit significant
performance drops when the train/test domains are not
consistent, which calls for further general robustness
investigations [12]. Recently proposed patch-interaction
models that rely on ViT (e.g., PV-ISM) showed transformers
can capture subtle patch-wise discrepancies and local texture
defects reflecting the synthetic nature of visual artifacts [13].
Furthermore, attention-enriched mobile architectures coupled
with the frequency cues showed that even small-footprint
models were capable of achieving high accuracy when details
in the spectrum are walked into their feature extraction
process [14]. An even more recent hybrid spatial–frequency
forensics framework demonstrated that joint learning of the
joint features performs better than detection in either domain
alone, and producing results that are robust against heavy
post-processing, resolution variation, and adversarial editing

[15].


Challenges In spite of the great strides made in deepfake
detection, there exists formidable research gaps that have
hampered the development and deployment of trusted
authentication systems out in the real world. Single-modality
detectors leverage a single form of cue (e.g., texture, motion,
or physiology). Making use of such reliance makes models
vulnerable unless the cue is corrupted by compression, postprocessing, light changes or it is explicitly removed when
synthesized by generators; thus single modality performance
degrades considerably in case of domain-shifts and
adversarial re-encoding. Current vision-based detectors are
mainly dedicated to unimodal cues (facial texture artifact,
physiological signal manipulation, or lip-sync mismatch),
leading to models that fall short in capturing deepfakes when
they become more realistic visually as well as when
fabricated via multimodal synthesis pipelines. Most existing
methods cannot handle such challenging scenarios as
illumination variations (especially low-light conditions),
strong compression from social media platforms, diverse
devices, as well as cross-dataset generalization. Additionally,
transformer-based models are most of the time not tailored
for joint integration of modalities as multimodal features
(RGB inconsistency/frequency domain perturbation/microexpressions/temporal dynamics) in a single forensic model.
Moreover, none of these works address the secure
authentication scenario with extremely low false-acceptance
rates, which are compulsory requirements for practical
deployments such as in financial/ government/enterprise

III. METHODOLOGY

To this end, we present a multi-modal deepfake-forensic
authentication pipeline for the simultaneous exploitation of
spatial, frequency and physiological signatures in the form of
a Multimodal Vision Transformer (MViT) architecture. In
contrast to most existing classification-based deepfake
detectors that only consider appearance, we extract RGBA,
high frequency artifacts,H and bio-rhythms cues, such as
remote photoplethysmography (rPPG),simultaneously. This
multimodal fusion guarantees high robustness even to
compressed, GAN-enhanced and diffusion-generated (or
adversarial-perturbed) fake samples. The ultimate goal is to
devise an authentication system resistant against state-of-theart spoofing, while ensuring trustable verification for static
image- and video-based identity checks.


_A._ _Dataset and Data Collection_

To control and compare with the public benchmarks, the
proposed work uses commonly used public datasets in
deepfake detection. The system is then trained and evaluated
based on:


1. **FaceForensics++ (FF++)** : a large-scale benchmark

of videos with facial forgeries created by FaceSwap,
DeepFakes, Face2Face and NeuralTextures released
free for academic use. It generates uncompressed
and compressed versions, making it possible to
evaluate robustness under a range of impairments.

2. **DeepFake Detection Challenge (DFDC)** : released

by Meta and Kaggle, this comprises high variability
in identities, illumination situations among other
conditions as well as compression and fostered
deepfake types. Its distribution as a real–fake and
insecure capture creates realistic testing for
authentication.


3. **Celeb-DF v2** : a clean dataset containing deepfake

videos free of glaring visual cues, and represent
more realistic fakes. This makes it useful for
evaluating generalization to high-fidelity synthesis
models.
4. **MMDF (Multimodal DeepFake Dataset:** used

especially for physiological signal extraction like
rPPG. The dataset contains the RGB frames and the
corresponding ground-truth physiological
waveforms, which is appropriate for testing multimodal fusion.

All the datasets are publicly available to researchers for
large-scale experimentations without licensing constraints.
Raw video frames, their corresponding frequency-domain
transform and physiological signal are extracted using the
same pipeline in order to unify data across different datasets.



_B._ _Proposed Algorithmic Framework_

The method is based on four main algorithmic building
blocks (Figure 1):


_1)_ _**Spatial Feature Encoder (SFE)**_
RGB frames are input to a Swin-Transformer based encoder
that includes the hierarchical spatial inconsistencese,
attention-based face morphology deviations and high levels
manipulative patterns. Patch-tokenization helps better
detecting localized errors around eyes, mouth, and boundary
blending areas – typical artifacts in diffusion and GAN-based
deepfakes. The Spatial Feature Encoder tokenizes the input
image into patches and uses hierarchical attention to encode
localized inconsistencies (boundary blending, eye/mouth
artefacts, and incorrect texture statistics); patch-wise hosting
weights are used to reveal which part of the 1lodulation has
anomalous gradients or witness artefacts typical for synthesis
pipelines


_2)_ _**Frequency Forensics Module (FFM)**_
Compression noise, blending residues and GAN-related
spectral fingerprints are captured by transforming the input
frame with a DCT and FFT. A lightweight ConvNeXt/Tiny
network is used to extract the frequency maps and produce
high-frequency anomaly embeddings. This branch greatly
increases robustness of high-quality deepfake models, which
can remove the visible spatial artifacts.


_3)_ _**Physiological Signal Extraction Module (PSEM)**_
A spatial-temporal attention network is designed to capture
remote PPG signals by the subtone color variations on
different facial parts. Genuine videos have clocked pulse
signals, whereas deepfakes contain defective or absent biosignals. The times series is fed into a 1-D Transformer to
obtain authentic embeddings of physiological features.


_4)_ _**Multimodal Vision Transformer (MViT) Fusion**_
The embdeddings from the SFE, FFM and PSEM branches
are first fused by cross-modal attention transformer. This
kind of fusion enables the network to co-attend:

  - spatial integrity,

  - anomalous behavior in the power spectra and/ or
apparent spectral index.

  - physiological coordination.


The model outputs a binary decision head which predicts
Real vs. Fake and an additional authentication confidence
head that produces a secure confidence score allowing
integration with access-control systems. The confidenceterminal-head produces a normalized authenticity score (01) that can be used by policies for decision threashHold’s,
risk-assessment stratification, and audit loggiNG; thresholds
may be adjusted to achieve desired false-acceptance / falserejection when traditionally client-deployed

IV. RESULT

The proposed Multimodal Vision Transformer Forensics
Network (MViT-Forensics) was benchmarked on four
popular datasets—FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF v2, DFDC
Preview and the Multimodal DeepFake Dataset (MMDF).
Together, these datasets collectively span a broad range of
deepfake manipulations, such as high-quality face swaps,
low-quality compressed fakes, multimodal inconsistencies
and physiological distortions. Experiments show that the
incorporation of such spatial–frequency–physiological
multimodal cues improves detection performance extensively,
and sensitive to very heavy compression with strong crossdataset generalization. The model superior to the transformeronly and CNN-based baselines, proving the necessity of
multimodal fusion for secure authentication.

_C._ _Discussion_

The comparative results are unequivocal, traditional
deepfake detectors being frustrated by the multimodality
discordance, high compression rates or cross-domain gap.
Cross-dataset generalization is required as the operating
conditions vary compared to laboratory setups (device
cameras, encoders, illumination, demographics). Strong
authentication criterion must be uniform through unseen
generator families, codecs and capture conditions in order to
not have blind spots where aggressive adversaries could
perform simple re-encoding or change of generator. Crossdataset evaluation is thus necessary for making realistic
security claims. In contrast, MViT-Forensics architecture
efficiently harnesses the synergistic character of several
modality (spatial textures, frequency-domain cues and
physiological rPPG signal) leading to a far more
discriminative representation and with better generalization
PriceIsRight. The MMDF outcomes also provide additional
evidence that physiological traits are strong factors for
biological identification, yet generative models have not
mastered to generate illusion. The excellent compression
robustness and the high cross-dataset performance illustrate
the capability of our proposed model for secure
authentication applications, in which deepfake intrusion has
to be detected with very low false positive rates. In general,
the results confirm that the proposed multimodal deepfakeresistant authentication is both effective and can be deployed
in practice.

V. CONCLUSION

In this work, we proposed a unified deepfake-resistant
authentication framework, namely Multimodal VisionTransformer Forensics (MViT-Forensics), that fuses spatial,
frequency and physiological (rPPG) signals through a
multimodal attention-based fusion mechanism. Extensive
experiments on four baseline benchmarks, including
FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF v2, DFDC and the multimodal
MMDF benchmark datasets, validate that our method
achieves consistently better accuracy as well as AUC and
demonstrates greater generalization ability and robustness
against heavy compression comparing with existing CNNand transformer-based methods. By leveraging
complementary forensic cues and jointly modeling them in a
transformer backbone, the system fills a large void among
academic deepfake detection and real-world authentication
robustness. The greater immunity to manipulation artifacts
and cross-dataset biases demonstrates its suitability for
secure identity-verification applications.


Future work extends to model adaptation for real-time
deployment on edge and mobile devices by architecture
optimization, distillation, and model compression. It is
possible to further build up its defence by improving
adversarial and anti-forensic resistance capabilities,
incorporating generator-aware augmentation as well as
performing continuous learning for ongoing maintenance of
keeping up with the fast-changing GAN-based and
diffusion-based deepfake methods. It is possible that the
multimodal scope can be extended with audio cues, depth or
infrared data and device level meta-data. Furthermore, the
privacy-preserving training methods like federated learning
and encrypted inference as well as explainable visualization
of attention maps and decision pathways could also
contribute to transparency, trust, and secure deployment in
high-security authentication systems.
